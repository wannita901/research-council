Research Framing & Evaluation Design

Drafted 2026-06-04 · Locked: ideation+verifier anchor · FSE/ICSE/ASE · v1 = Core + bias (RQ1–RQ3)

The platform is the artifact; the paper is a controlled study. The thesis is falsifiable: heterogeneous cross-vendor debate, grounded in executable verification, produces better AI4SE research artifacts than a single strong model or a same-vendor panel. If that's false, a clean design still yields a publishable, honest negative result.

Status update (2026-06-05) — pivoted to product-first

The project pivoted to building a working agentic product; this controlled-eval framing is now optional / later, not the design driver. Full tracing is kept so we can still evaluate or publish if we choose. See Product Re-grounding (Agentic v2).

The trap to avoid

"We built a multi-agent system" is not a contribution at ICSE/FSE. The contribution is evidence that isolates why it works — specifically that vendor heterogeneity (not just more compute) and verification grounding (not just debate) each carry weight. The baseline ladder below is engineered to prove exactly that, one rung at a time.

1. Research questions & hypotheses

RQ1 · Heterogeneity
Does a heterogeneous cross-vendor peer panel produce higher-quality AI4SE research artifacts than (a) the strongest single model and (b) a homogeneous same-vendor panel of equal size and budget?
H1: heterogeneous > homogeneous > single — diverse vendors have complementary error profiles, so cross-critique catches more flaws. The homogeneous-panel baseline is what separates "diversity helps" from "more samples help."
RQ2 · Verification grounding
Does grounding the debate in executable verification improve the feasibility / correctness of artifacts relative to debate without verification?
H2: verification-grounded debate raises objective feasibility without sacrificing novelty — directly narrowing the documented ideation–execution gap (LLM ideas: novel but infeasible).
RQ3 · Self-preference bias
Do peers favour their own candidates when judging, and does authorship anonymization reduce it?
H3: de-anonymized judging shows measurable self-preference; anonymization significantly reduces it. A clean, novel, measurable result regardless of RQ1's outcome.
RQ4 · Cost–quality
What is the cost / latency vs. quality tradeoff of the panel against single-model baselines?
Descriptive (no directional hypothesis) — reviewers expect an honest accounting of the price of the gains.

3. Evaluation task — vision-aligned and objectively grounded

The elegant move: evaluate on the product's actual use case (AI4SE research ideation) but make the hardest-to-fake axis — feasibility — objective via the verifier. Given an AI4SE topic, the panel outputs a research idea + a minimal experiment plan; the verifier then checks whether that plan's scaffold actually compiles and runs. So "feasibility" is measured, not just rated — which is exactly the RQ2 contribution, embedded in the real workflow.

Primary · objective

Verifier feasibility pass rate: does the proposed minimal experiment scaffold compile / run / emit a metric? Fully automatable.

Primary · expert

Blind expert Likert on novelty / soundness / overall, replicating Si et al. 2024. Report Krippendorff's α (inter-rater reliability).

Mechanism

Proposal diversity (embedding distance among the 3 independent proposals); self-preference rate; rounds-to-converge.

Practical

USD cost / run, wall-clock latency, tokens.

Optional robustness tier: a fully objective AI4SE task (program repair / test generation, e.g. a SWE-bench-Verified or Defects4J slice) where quality = % resolved — no human raters, hardens the RQ1/RQ2 claims.

4. Statistical rigor

  • Multiple seeds per condition (decoding is stochastic at temp > 0); report mean ± bootstrap CI.
  • Paired tests across conditions on the same tasks: McNemar for pass/fail, Wilcoxon signed-rank for ratings.
  • Multiple-comparison correction (Holm) across the baseline ladder; report effect sizes (Cliff's δ), not just p-values.
  • Pre-register RQs, conditions, and metrics before the full run (credibility + guards against p-hacking).

5. Data & contamination #1 reviewer attack on LLM-for-code

  • Use topics / benchmark tasks dated after the models' training cutoffs, or freshly authored items, to limit memorization.
  • Report each model's cutoff date and run a contamination probe (e.g., can the model recite held-out content?).
  • Verification grounding partly defends here: a memorized-but-wrong idea still fails the executable check.
  • Wiki snapshot: the knowledge wiki is git-versioned; the commit hash is recorded in every run trace, so each result is tied to an exact, auditable knowledge state.
  • Retrieval tool-set: the enabled sources (wiki/openalex/arxiv/…) are fixed across all conditions in a batch and logged per run; web search is disabled in eval (non-reproducible).

6. Threats to validity

Construct
Do pass rate + Likert capture "research quality"? Mitigate by triangulating objective feasibility, expert novelty/soundness, and (optionally) downstream execution. State the gap honestly.
Internal
Contamination; prompt sensitivity (report prompts, test paraphrases); the human-confirm step risks experimenter bias — keep the primary objective metric independent of the human so the headline result isn't confounded by it.
External
Three specific vendors at one moment in time (models change fast — version-pin and date everything); single domain (AI4SE); generalization across lifecycle stages is future work, not claimed.
Conclusion
Stochastic-decoding variance (seeds + CIs); multiple comparisons (correction); cost-matched conditions so significance isn't a budget artifact.

7. Contribution claims (precise)

  1. The first system + controlled study of heterogeneous cross-vendor debate grounded in executable verification for AI4SE research tasks.
  2. Evidence isolating the effect of vendor heterogeneity (vs. a cost-matched homogeneous panel) and of verification grounding (vs. ungrounded debate), via a baseline ladder + ablations.
  3. A measurable account of self-preference bias in cross-vendor panels and the effect of authorship anonymization — new, and independently interesting.
  4. An open, provider-agnostic platform (artifact) for reproducible multi-LLM research orchestration.
