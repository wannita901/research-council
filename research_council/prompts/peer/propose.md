You are {codename}. Turn your research gap into a concrete, defensible RESEARCH PROPOSAL the
council will argue over. Fill every field, specifically and honestly:

- `title` — specific and informative.
- `problem_statement` — the concrete problem you're solving (1–2 sentences).
- `motivation` — why it matters now: the impact / who benefits.
- `hypothesis` — the testable claim.
- `method` — the proposed approach, concretely enough to implement.
- `experiment_plan` — the OVERALL step-by-step plan (numbered), smallest runnable step first;
  name the baseline(s) to compare against.
- `research_questions` — break the work into 1–3 concrete research questions (RQ1, RQ2, …).
  For EACH, give the `question`, a focused step-by-step `plan` to answer it, and the `metrics`
  that answer it. Each RQ becomes its own experiment in Stage B, so keep them independently
  runnable and small.
- `dataset_metrics` — the dataset(s) (real or a clearly-described synthetic stand-in) and the
  overall evaluation metric(s) that would confirm or refute the hypothesis.
- `fallback_plan` — what you'll do if the main plan fails (a simpler variant, an ablation, or a
  different angle) so the project still yields a result.

Evidence rule (ALWAYS): every factual claim — especially in `problem_statement` and
`motivation` (e.g. "X is slow", "no prior work does Y") — must be backed by a reference from
your research brief, cited inline as `[ref-id]`. If you have no reference for a claim, either
drop it or restate it as an explicit assumption/hypothesis to be tested — never assert an
unsupported fact. Numbers and "state of the art" claims especially need a citation.

Be specific and avoid hand-waving — peers will critique soundness and feasibility and will
challenge any uncited claim, and you may revise any field during the discussion.
